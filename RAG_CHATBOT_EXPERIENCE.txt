I
 Thought Building a RAG Chatbot Was Easy. Then I Started Working With Real PDFs.


A few months ago, if someone had asked me how to build a RAG chatbot over PDFs, my answer would have been pretty straightforward:

Extract text
Create embeddings
Store them in a vector database
Retrieve relevant chunks
Let an LLM answer questions

Simple, right?

Then I started working with actual documents.

Not clean PDFs from tutorials. Real documents.

Some pages were scanned images. Some contained large tables. Others had diagrams, charts, title blocks, headers, footers, and layouts that completely confused normal text extractors.

That's when I realized that RAG is not really a retrieval problem.

It's a document understanding problem.

What Worked For Me During Document Extraction
I experimented with multiple tools for extracting content from PDFs.

PyMuPDF — 9/10
This became one of my favorites.

It is fast, lightweight, and gives good control over text, page information, coordinates, and metadata.

For documents that already contain machine-readable text, PyMuPDF handled most cases surprisingly well.

pdfplumber — 8/10
I found pdfplumber particularly useful when I needed more control over layout information and table extraction.

The output is usually cleaner than many basic PDF readers, although it can become slower on larger files.

Unstructured — 8.5/10
I liked the fact that it tries to understand document structure rather than simply extracting text.

For RAG pipelines, preserving headings and sections can be extremely valuable.

The tradeoff is that it adds some complexity compared to simpler extraction libraries.

OCR: When PDFs Aren't Really PDFs
One thing that surprised me was how many PDFs are actually just images.

Text extraction completely fails in those cases.

Tesseract OCR — 7.5/10
A great free option.

Works well for clean scans.

Accuracy starts dropping when documents contain low-quality scans, stamps, handwritten notes, or complex layouts.

AWS Textract — 9/10
Much better at handling real-world business documents.

I was impressed by its ability to understand forms and tables.

The downside is cost when processing large volumes of documents.

Google Document AI — 9/10
Probably one of the most complete document understanding solutions I explored.

Strong OCR performance and excellent handling of structured documents.

Again, cost becomes a factor at scale.

Tables Were Harder Than I Expected
Tables turned out to be one of the biggest challenges.

Converting a table into plain text often destroys the relationships between rows and columns.

Camelot — 8.5/10
Worked surprisingly well on clean, digitally generated PDFs.

For structured reports, it saved a lot of effort.

Tabula — 7.5/10
Easy to use and useful for quick extraction.

I found Camelot slightly more reliable in many cases.

Document AI / Textract Tables — 9/10
When tables became complex, cloud-based document AI tools generally performed much better than traditional extraction libraries.

Understanding Charts and Images
Another thing most RAG tutorials ignore is charts.

A graph can contain critical information, yet traditional pipelines completely skip it.

I experimented with multimodal models to generate descriptions and extract insights from charts.

GPT-4o — 9/10
Strong reasoning and good at describing charts and diagrams.

Gemini — 8.5/10
Very capable for visual understanding tasks and document analysis.

Claude Vision — 8.5/10
I liked the quality of explanations, especially for complex visuals.

One lesson I learned here:

A chart should not only be summarized.

If possible, extract actual values, labels, and trends so they become searchable later.

Embeddings and Vector Databases
For embeddings, I explored:

OpenAI Embeddings
BGE
E5

My experience was that chunk quality often mattered more than the embedding model itself.

A great embedding model cannot fix poorly structured chunks.

For storage, I looked at:

Pinecone
Weaviate
ChromaDB

ChromaDB — 9/10
Very easy to get started with.

Perfect for experiments and local development.

Pinecone — 9/10
Great managed experience and scales well.

Weaviate — 8.5/10
Powerful and flexible, especially for larger deployments.

The Biggest Lesson
The biggest mistake I made initially was focusing too much on embeddings and vector databases.

After working with real documents, I realized that most retrieval problems actually start much earlier in the pipeline.

If OCR is poor, retrieval suffers.

If tables are flattened into text, retrieval suffers.

If charts are ignored, retrieval suffers.

If chunking breaks context, retrieval suffers.

By the time the query reaches the vector database, many mistakes have already been made.

Today, if someone asks me how to improve a RAG system, my first question is no longer:

"What embedding model are you using?"

It's:

"How are you processing your documents?"

Because that's usually where the real gains come from.
